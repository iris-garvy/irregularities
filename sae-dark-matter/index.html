SAE dark matter

Sparse-autoencoder reconstruction errors contain predictable structure. Engels et al., 2024 ↗

In brief

When sparse autoencoders (SAEs) rebuild a model's activations, a substantial share of the leftover error is linearly predictable from those activations. One independent preprint reproduced this on Gemma-2-9b, without reported controls or uncertainty. Error persists even in the largest SAEs tested, but it is unproven that some always remains. SAE error can harm model behavior more than equally large random noise, but this is contested: the result depends on how the noise is structured. Whether the error is mostly a training artifact is also contested, and whether its predictable part contains features the SAE missed remains open.

Papers to start with: Engels et al., 2024; O'Neill et al., 2025; wesg, 2024

Intervention Types; Experiments and Results

Related findings3 groups of studies

Results that bear on this claim without testing it as worded.

Reconstruction depends on SAE design and optimization 13 studies

The encoder's flexibility, the directions of learned features, constraints on sparse outputs and optimization all affect reconstruction error. Their relative importance varies across settings.

  • iSAE-ME sharply reduces reconstruction error on noiseless sparse-linear synthetic data. Walter Nelson et al., 2026 ↗
Host representations affect reconstruction difficulty 4 studies

Reconstruction difficulty depends on the host representation's geometry, architecture and training, as well as the SAE.

  • At matched feature capacity and sparsity, MoWE feedforward updates reconstruct more accurately than dense feedforward updates. Tavish Mankash et al., 2026 ↗
Fewer features that never activate need not improve fidelity 3 studies

Reducing the number of features that never activate, or preventing feature activation strengths from being reduced, does not by itself guarantee lower reconstruction error or downstream loss.

  • On SONNY, B-spline curves replace ReLU, reducing features that never activate but capturing less activation variance. Minjong Cheon, 2026 ↗

Checking input coverage

Inspect reconstruction across token identities, frequencies and input conditions to uncover failures hidden by aggregate metrics.

Reconstruction error varies across inputs 9 studies

Reconstruction error varies across token types, positions, contexts, languages and formatting. Aggregate fidelity can hide these differences, and larger error need not imply poorer model capability.

  • Reconstruction quality and downstream cross-entropy changes worsen beyond the SAE's training context, especially in early layers. Evan Anders, 2024 ↗
Token identity and frequency shape error 3 studies

Token frequency and identity explain some reconstruction error, and token-specific biases can absorb part of it. The SAE reduces feature activation strengths by different amounts depending on how often and strongly features activate.

  • A token-specific decoder bias reduces added cross-entropy across the tested Pythia-1.4B layers. tdooms, 2024 ↗

Choosing and adapting SAEs

Choose sparsity settings, training objectives and adaptation methods to suit the model representations being studied. Also consider how replacing them with SAE reconstructions affects the model's later behavior.

Transfer depends on compatible representations 7 studies

Transferring an SAE across layers, seeds, checkpoints and fine-tuning stages depends on how well their representations match. Aligning representations or training on the target representations can improve otherwise unsuccessful transfer.

  • A fixed CLIP SAE transfers poorly after standard fine-tuning; L2 regularization or SAE-FT improves fidelity. Fabian Morelli et al., 2026 ↗
Sparsity affects reconstruction and downstream fidelity 4 studies

The number of active features allowed and the sparsity penalty can govern reconstruction accuracy and the cost to downstream model behavior. Improvements sometimes require codes with more active features.

  • Larger k improves SMI-TED fidelity; dictionary expansion minimally affects changes in loss at matched sparsity. J. Cohen et al., 2025 ↗
Adaptation and training choices change replacement cost 3 studies

Adaptation, training objectives and sparsity implementations can change the functional cost of SAE reconstruction. Gains depend on the model and configuration.

  • Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) reduces cross-entropy when multiple Llama Scope SAEs jointly replace activations. Matthew Chen et al., 2025 ↗

Evaluating SAE utility

Evaluate how well behavior and information are preserved, how interpretable the features are, and how effective interventions are, alongside reconstruction metrics.

Activation fidelity does not guarantee preserved behavior 7 studies

Activation reconstruction metrics do not reliably predict whether loss, task performance or steering effectiveness will be preserved. Some poorly reconstructed directions have little importance for model behavior.

  • π0.5 mean-pool SAEs preserve activation fidelity but sharply reduce success, without matched random-error controls. Bryce Grant et al., 2026 ↗
Feature interventions depend on the setting 7 studies

SAE features can support causal interventions and steering. Their effectiveness depends on behavior, seed, sparsity and intervention design, and need not exceed simple dense baselines.

  • Amplifying the top-10 SAE feature activations from PatchTST's feedforward network by 5× shifts forecasts. Alper Yıldırım, 2026 ↗
Information preservation depends on the task 5 studies

Sparse codes and reconstructions retain some information that linear models can read, while losing information needed for other tasks. Comparing probes alone does not explain how reconstruction error carries information.

  • Reconstructed vectors underperform dense vectors on held-out cross-architecture retrieval at both tested anchor settings. M. Wenzel, 2026 ↗
Fidelity and interpretability can diverge 4 studies

Better reconstruction or downstream loss does not guarantee more faithful or interpretable features. Feature interpretability also need not track causal importance.

  • Combining toy-model features gives lower reconstruction error than keeping only some of the correct features. chanind, 2025 ↗
Low-rank components can carry important information 2 studies

Low-rank channels can capture causally important information or correct part of reconstruction-induced loss. Learned scaffolds and corrective adapters are different objects.

  • Removing a learned 24-dimensional component raises cross-entropy more than removing the top-24 principal components. Rui-Xuan Deng et al., 2026 ↗

Known gaps in this draft

Independent support, source accuracy, missing evidence and experimental coverage remain uncertain, limiting how broadly the findings can be generalized.

All 6 gaps
  • Independent authorship and shared experiments have not been checked across studies grouped under the same theme. Apparent agreement may therefore overstate independent support.
