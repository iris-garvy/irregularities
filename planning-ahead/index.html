Known gaps in this draft

The main gaps concern independent replication, verified quantitative evidence, general-text prediction and causal tests separating future plans from input conditioning or shared computation.

All 7 gaps
  • The original Claude rhyme evidence comes from one couplet, without aggregate success rates, uncertainty estimates or a detailed control protocol. Generalization across prompts, simultaneous candidate words and other frontier models remains untested.
  • Exact-token prediction two to four positions ahead in general text lacks replication beyond GPT-J against unigram or bigram baselines. Findings from constrained contexts do not fill this gap.
  • Rhyme interventions at the last input word may alter a future plan or simply change conditioning on that word. Testing whether patching changes the intermediate words would help distinguish these explanations.
  • Independent replication within the same model and task remains uncertain, including unresolved author overlap among studies. Convergent findings across rhyme, general text and graph tasks do not establish replication of each setting.
  • Key numerical results for rhyme patching, steering and probing still need verification against the source tables. Some newline-steering evidence is reported only in prose, limiting quantitative comparison.
  • Information irrelevant to immediate prediction may support later predictions or arise through circuit sharing. Conflicting myopic-training results still lack a quantitative independent replication.
  • Bridge-concept interventions change answer rankings, but changes in generated answers remain unestablished. Evidence for answers preceding visible reasoning also depends on model and language, and truncation scores do not measure empty-trace accuracy.
