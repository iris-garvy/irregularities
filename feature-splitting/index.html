V1As SAE dictionaries grow, coarse features split into finer, nearby features 1490

Suggested status R2independently reproduced

Independent groups see the pattern in Llama-3.1-8B, speaker and astronomy-text embeddings, and small vision and language models, but mostly for hand-picked features; much of the other support shares an author with an original study. Splitting is often partial: larger SAEs also add new features unlike any in smaller ones, and finer features nest only partly under coarse ones across sizes; no study directly measures whether the finer features together fire wherever the coarse one did.

Original resultBricken et al., 2023 ↗

They compared SAEs of several dictionary sizes, from 512 features upward, trained on a one-layer transformer.

One base64 feature in the 512-feature SAE splits into three in the 4,096-feature SAE (letters, digits, encoded ASCII text), which together seem to fire wherever it did, and similar features have small angles between their decoder vectors. The evidence is qualitative, and features can also merge, so it is not a simple tree.

Larger dictionaries can add dead, rare or insensitive latents 7 studies

Enlarging an SAE dictionary can add mostly dead, rarer-firing or less input-sensitive latents rather than more concept-specific or functionally relevant ones. Several of these results come from non-language models.

  • Average feature sensitivity falls as SAE width grows, across GemmaScope and seven SAEBench architectures. Claire Tian et al., 2025 ↗
  • In a cardiac model, wider SAEs reach 70% dead latents while phenotype-specific latents stay flat. Yi-Xuan Duan et al., 2026 ↗
  • Spliced into PatchTST, 0.5x-4.0x SAEs hurt forecasting about equally; on Traffic, 4.0x has 54.9-65.8% dead latents. Alper Yıldırım, 2026 ↗
Single-latent capture of a concept depends on width and layer 7 studies

Whether a single latent captures a concept for probing, steering or sense separation depends on dictionary width and layer, sometimes peaking at an intermediate width. At larger widths a sum of several latents can be needed to recover a coarse concept, and single latents remain worse classifiers than linear probes.

  • Summing 65K-SAE toxic latents cuts their steering gap to the 16K SAE from 0.062 to 0.01. Agam Goyal et al., 2025 ↗
  • Separate Liberalism and Conservatism latents appear at 131k but at 16k only in layer 31. Shu Yang et al., 2024 ↗
  • In GPT-2 small, PS-Eval word-sense accuracy rises with SAE width, saturating near 64x at layer 4. Gouki Minegishi et al., 2025 ↗
SAEs tile continuous manifolds with many localized latents 4 studies

SAEs represent continuous feature manifolds by tiling them with many localized latents or by splitting them across blocks. How far this goes depends on manifold geometry, the sparsity budget and the architecture, not only on dictionary size.

  • SAEs tile toy manifolds with many latents, and how loss scales depends on manifold geometry. Eric J. Michaud et al., 2025 ↗
  • In Llama3.1-8B, SAEs fragment manifolds across many partially redundant localized features. Usha Bhalla et al., 2026 ↗
  • Block-sparse featurizers split features across blocks even on independent data, worse at higher top-k. Alexandru-Iulius Jerpelea et al., 2026 ↗
Feature granularity depends on sparsity setting, not only width 3 studies

At equal reconstruction error, feature granularity depends on the sparsity setting (L0, the number of active features, or an L1 penalty) as well as on dictionary width. Choosing the SAE by minimum description length (MDL) can select an intermediate granularity.

  • Raising an InceptionV1 SAE's L1 penalty shifts activation from a double-curve feature to single-curve features. Liv Gorton, 2024 ↗
  • The author states that sparser CLIP-embedding SAEs represent each concept with fewer, more generic features. Gytis Daujotas, 2024 ↗
  • On MNIST at equal error, width and L0 set feature type; description-length minimization picks stroke-like features. K. Ayonrinde et al., 2024 ↗

Suggested status R2independently reproduced

Independent toy, theory and objective-comparison studies find that standard sparse objectives produce absorption or splitting on hierarchical inputs, and that Matryoshka and other objectives cut absorption on Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3-8B. For splitting itself in models of this size, most reported drops come from overlapping author groups and the one independent test gives mixed results; no study at any scale tests whether splitting reflects the SAE rather than the model's own units.

Original resultChanin et al., 2024 ↗

They trained SAEs on toy models with four orthogonal features or twelve features, varying whether a child feature fires only with its parent.

With independent features the SAE recovered one latent per feature, but when the child fired only with its parent, the child latent absorbed the parent direction and the parent latent failed to fire on the child's inputs. The pattern weakened when the child co-occurred with its parent 95% of the time and still appeared in a batch TopK SAE (k=2) with no L1 term; the paper measured absorption, not splitting, and tested no Matryoshka-style objective.

Related findings4 groups of studies

Results that bear on this claim without testing it as worded.

One concept spread over several latents at fixed size 10 studies

At a fixed dictionary size, SAEs can spread one concept over several partly redundant latents: near-duplicates, near-collinear decoder clusters, spatial fragments or many weak latents. Supervision or spatial priors can concentrate the concept, and redundancy can grow with dictionary size in a way that depends on the architecture.

  • GPT-2 Small SAEs trained on task data spread one binary attribute over ten-plus hard-to-interpret features. Aleksandar Makelov et al., 2024 ↗
  • Open GPT-2 and Mistral-7B SAE decoders contain clusters of near-collinear vectors. Seyed Arshan Dalili et al., 2026 ↗
  • In Stable Diffusion v1.5, unsupervised SAE concepts are dispersed; supervision yields one dominant latent per concept. E. Cassano et al., 2025 ↗
Low-loss SAE solutions can differ from the true features 6 studies

Good reconstruction does not certify feature recovery: theory, toy models and checks on real SAEs show that SAE objectives admit low-loss solutions other than the true features. These include spurious polysemantic minima, a diffuse phase, composed directions, several equally sparse codes for the same data, and spurious hierarchies on off-distribution inputs.

  • Vanilla L1 SAEs on dense synthetic data reconstruct near-perfectly but recover few true features. Alexis D. Plascencia, 2026 ↗
  • A GPT2-small SAE violates optimality on French/German Wikipedia and code, mostly hierarchically, but not on OpenWebText. William Dorrell, 2026 ↗
  • Three Gemma Scope latents are nearly linearly dependent, giving two minimal codes with different explanations. Kola Ayonrinde, 2024 ↗
Matryoshka-type objectives order latents from broad to specific 5 studies

Nested-prefix (Matryoshka-type) objectives order latents from broad to specific within one SAE and cluster decoder geometry by granularity level, and ordered variants improve cross-seed ordering. Nesting between prefix groups looks like part-whole refinement rather than splitting.

  • An ordered Matryoshka variant makes latent order more seed-consistent, though its overall stability is sometimes lower. Sophie L. Wang et al., 2025 ↗
  • On Amazon Fashion embeddings, Matryoshka latents run from broad to more differentiated with index. Yagel Alfasi et al., 2026 ↗
  • Nested Matryoshka children cover a few percent of parent inputs, an asymmetry authors read as part-whole. Madelyn Mathai et al., 2026 ↗
Opposite concepts may split into two non-negative SAE latents 2 studies

SAEs with non-negative activations may split opposite (antipodal) concepts into two latents; signed latents can merge them in toy models with little superposition. In real SAEs, however, the two sides of a signed latent or a latent pair rarely form a clear, nameable opposition.

  • One signed AbsTopK SAE feature is positive for 'man', negative for 'woman', in otherwise identical inputs. Xudong Zhu et al., 2025 ↗
  • Signed latents merge antipodal pairs in low-superposition toys, but in audited real latents coherent oppositions are no more common than in random pairings. Bartosz Wieciech et al., 2026 ↗

Checking SAE latents and metrics

Findings on whether a trained SAE's latents and its absorption or splitting scores can be trusted before they are used as evidence.

Absorption and splitting metrics may be confounded or weakly valid 5 studies

Instance-level measures of absorption and splitting can be confounded or only weakly valid; examples are top-1 firing consensus, ablation-effect similarity and the SAEBench Splitting score. Proposed alternative detectors use activation-histogram peaks or rule-surrogate similarity, which compares rules fitted to mimic each latent.

  • A preregistered absorption proxy based on firing consensus fails its validity threshold on Pythia SAEs. Jin-Yuan Zhang et al., 2026 ↗
  • Ablation-effect similarity is not bimodal and does not strongly track judged absorption. Theodore Ehrenborg, 2025 ↗
  • Comparing rule surrogates flags ClimaX SAE latent pairs with near-identical rules; the method picked the examples. Hugo Porta et al., 2026 ↗
Artifact latents from optimisation or data statistics 4 studies

Some SAE latents reflect optimisation or data-statistics artifacts rather than model concepts. Examples are dense position or first-token latents that recur across sizes, unigram reconstructors and position-locked latents.

  • Dense position, first-token and repetition latents recur across differently trained GPT-2 small SAEs. Leo Gao et al., 2024 ↗
  • An estimated 35-45% of features reconstruct common unigrams; a lookup bias cuts this below 5%. Thomas Dooms et al., 2025 ↗
  • Some diffusion-model TopK SAE latents fire in fixed spatial regions regardless of semantics. Berk Tınaz et al., 2025 ↗

Steering with SAE latents

Findings on how reliably SAE latent directions work as causal or steering handles in practice.

SAE feature directions are uneven steering and causal handles 7 studies

SAE feature directions are uneven causal and steering handles. Some generalise or separate tasks well, while others behave like random directions under perturbation, fail to steer, or interfere when composed.

  • Two of ten SAE features picked by honest-vs-dishonest contrast detect impostors well; top-feature steering fails. Satvik Golechha et al., 2025 ↗
  • In Gemma-2 9B, task-relevant SAE directions separate by task, while important MLP neurons do not. Evan Ning et al., 2026 ↗
  • Under perturbation, SAE-feature directions mostly behave like random directions, unlike contrastive difference-of-means directions. Francisco Ferreira da Silva, 2026 ↗

Across models, layers and languages

Findings on how SAE features correspond or change across models, finetunes, layers, checkpoints and languages, which matters when reusing or comparing dictionaries.

SAE features partly correspond across models, finetunes and domains 7 studies

SAE features partly correspond across models, finetunes, training-data domains, architectures and weight pruning. How much they correspond depends on the model pair and the matching method.

  • Base-model SAE reconstruction collapses on a Gemma-2b code finetune but holds up on Mistral-7B MetaMath. Taras Kutsyk, 2024 ↗
  • In PaliGemma 2 dictionary matching, 96.41% of features are preserved and 0.23% are emergent. Hunar Batra et al., 2026 ↗
  • Nearly all Mamba-130m features have a Pythia-70m match above Jaccard 0.35, with no null baseline. Rithin Nagaraj et al., 2026 ↗
Features grow more abstract with depth and over pretraining 5 studies

SAE features become broader or more abstract with layer depth and over pretraining. The granularity a dictionary finds therefore depends on the layer and the checkpoint.

  • In a clinical sequence model, singleton SAE features fall from 45.7% to 0.5% across layers. Chris Sainsbury et al., 2026 ↗
  • In DINOv2, 90 layer-36 SAE heads fully cover ontology categories; a layer-24 head covers only 0.119. M. Olson et al., 2025 ↗
  • Over Pythia-1B pretraining, top crosscoder features shift from token-level to abstract grammatical detectors. Deniz Bayazit et al., 2025 ↗
Duplicate latents across languages, scripts and modalities 4 studies

Studies differ on whether SAEs keep separate latents for the same concept across languages, scripts or modalities. Standard SAEs may learn language- or modality-specific duplicates, while script variants of one sentence share many latents, more so at larger model scale.

  • Highly coactivated image-text SAE feature pairs in VLMs do not share near-identical directions. Chung-Pa Lee et al., 2026 ↗
  • Serbian Latin and Cyrillic sentences share more Gemma-3 SAE features as model scale grows. Sripad Karne, 2026 ↗
  • H-SAE top features for translated tokens differ less across languages than standard SAE features. Mark Muchane et al., 2025 ↗
SAE features recur and persist across layers 4 studies

SAE features recur across nearby layers: their decoder directions stay near-identical or their activation patterns persist. They can be traced as paths across layers, along which a feature persists, merges with others or narrows to a more specific meaning.

  • Three bishop-movement features in different chess-model layers have highly similar decoders, which authors call cross-layer superposition. Rui Lin et al., 2026 ↗
  • On deeper layers, a layer-24 SAE keeps many of the same features active (adjacent-layer carry-over 0.72-0.82). V. Menon et al., 2026 ↗
  • Per-layer Pythia SAE features best match the Group-SAE trained on the layer group containing their layer. Davide Ghilardi et al., 2024 ↗

Known gaps in this draft

The evidence for feature splitting leans on a few overlapping groups, small or unstated model sizes and within-dictionary measures, and other explanations of what larger SAEs add have not been weighed against splitting.

All 7 gaps
  • Much of the real-model evidence for splitting and absorption comes from a few groups that overlap with the original authors, and some results appear twice, as a paper and as a blog post. Independent replication is thinner than the number of studies suggests.
  • Direct evidence that features split as open-release SAEs grow rests mainly on one hand-picked Llama-3.1-8B feature at one layer. Gemma Scope width series appear in other studies, but mostly for absorption or sensitivity rather than splitting itself.
  • Parent-child families have been measured inside a single dictionary. No study tests quantitatively whether features that split across dictionary sizes form such families.
  • Matryoshka-type and other objectives lower measured absorption in several studies, but independent evidence that they lower splitting in real models is weak. One independent study finds no change in split counts on Qwen3-8B, overlapping error intervals on Gemma-2-2B and single runs elsewhere, and its baseline for one setting is reported with two different values.
  • Absorption rates vary widely by model and setting, for example 0.0074 on Qwen3-8B vs 0.1908 on Gemma-2-2B in one study, and two studies on the same Gemma-2-2B layer and width, at different L0, report BatchTopK values of 0.49 and 0.19 that have not been reconciled. Only the original study tests absorption causally, by ablation.
  • Larger dictionaries may add dead, rare or insensitive latents, or tile continuous manifolds, rather than split coarse features into finer ones. These alternative readings have not been weighed against splitting.
  • No splitting or absorption test covers models above 10B parameters; larger models appear only in side results, such as steering on Llama-3.3-70b and script sharing on Gemma-3 up to 27B. Only one study varies width on an instruction-tuned model, many studies do not state model size, and the decomposition of large-SAE latents into smaller-SAE latents is tested only on GPT-2 Small.
