Joint ablation effects are not additive 5 studies

Joint ablations can produce effects that differ from the sum of separate removals. These interactions complicate component rankings based on isolated interventions.

  • Jointly ablating selected attention scores changes outputs more than the sum of separate ablations. Joakim Edin et al., 2025 ↗
  • Cross-layer interactions are substantial, while joint head–MLP effects deviate from additivity in either direction. Abdallah Khemais, 2026 ↗
  • Biological transformers show non-additive patching effects that sharply reduce certifiable rankings under structural-bias assumptions. Ihor Kendiukhov, 2026 ↗
Downstream responses complicate attribution 4 studies

Downstream erasure and backup behavior complicate direct logit attribution and single-step patching. Blocking compensation changes head rankings and circuit metrics in ways that depend on the model.

  • L0H2’s direct logit attribution remains high with unrelated inputs and anticorrelates with downstream erasers. Jett Janiak et al., 2023 ↗
  • Repeated patching lets a backup restore speed despite immediate braking in a toy robot. Gihyuk Ko, 2026 ↗
  • Blocking downstream compensation changes head rankings, with model-dependent effects on circuit metrics. Sankaran Vaidyanathan et al., 2026 ↗
Ablation can overstate component importance 2 studies

Ablation, retraining, and observational estimates can assign different importance to components. Sequence-pooled normalization can make removal substantially more damaging than training without the same blocks.

  • Sequence-pooled normalization makes long-range block ablation much more damaging than retraining without those blocks. Qing Tian, 2026 ↗
  • Interaction scores correlate with knockout effects; many claimed IOI components lose coverage when the task disappears. Po-Kai Chen et al., 2026 ↗

Known gaps in this draft

The main gaps concern mechanism identification, independent replication, contributor shares, matched ablation comparisons, measurement scope, attribution causality, and evidence verification.

All 7 gaps
  • Small ablation effects and overcorrection do not uniquely identify active compensation. Redundant pathways and proportional downstream counter-responses remain alternative explanations.
  • Independent replication of head-level self-repair on general text remains limited. Existing GPT-2 Small evidence with shared authors does not establish broad replication, while prompt variability and overcorrection still need further testing.
  • The combined share of repair carried by backup heads, LayerNorm, and anti-erasure neurons remains untested. Identifying contributors does not establish how much total recovery they explain.
  • No independent group has compared restored-output fractions across matched zero, mean, and resample ablations. Whole-layer mean ablation remains untested, and whether particular mean replacements preserve natural activation geometry remains unresolved.
  • Selected-neuron swaps measured on binary decision margins do not establish whole-layer restoration of the most likely token. Some supporting layer-ablation results measure only the next layer’s predictive influence.
  • No study isolates self-repair as the cause of misleading component-importance estimates. Redundancy, downstream erasure, and shared normalization paths offer other explanations.
  • Some supporting evidence remains incompletely verified, including the definitions separating repair from redundancy in tabular models. Independence among later studies also remains unestablished.
