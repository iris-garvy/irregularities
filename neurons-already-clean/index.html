Most neurons are already interpretable ("neural networks are already clean")

A claim that most neurons have coherent, identifiable roles. Cammarata, 2026 ↗

In brief

An essay argues that most neurons already have one main job, but its headline figure has no independent check and outside evidence thins above 10B parameters. That figure, about 90% in Qwen3-32B, comes from a preliminary audit that counted each neuron's positive and negative sides separately. The closest outside test counted whole neurons in a different model, Qwen-2.5-32B, and found one consistent explanation for only 67.1%. Other groups confirm that architecture, regularization and analysis tools change how interpretable neurons look, though layer width matters too. They also find that explanations fitting a neuron's top-activating examples can mispredict its firing.

Papers to start with: Cammarata, 2026; Bills et al., 2023; Ward et al., 2025

Many language-model neurons carry several behaviours 24 studies

In small-to-mid language models, many MLP neurons, especially in early layers, show several behaviours, fire on unrelated content or resist labelling. How large the polysemantic share is depends strongly on the measure.

  • In Gemma, Qwen and Llama models, each frequently active neuron's explanations form 2.2–5.5 clusters on average. Wei-Qi Liu et al., 2026 ↗
  • Strongly connected neurons that are polysemantic make up under 5% of each Pythia-70M and GPT-2-Small layer. Bo Gong et al., 2025 ↗
  • Many early-layer Pythia neurons fire on many unrelated n-grams, suggesting heavier superposition. wesg, 2023 ↗
Interpretable-unit shares outside language models vary widely 22 studies

Outside language models, estimated shares of interpretable units vary widely by model, layer and method. They range from most units in some ResNet late layers, and about two thirds in others, to under 1% in a tabular foundation model tested against predefined concepts.

  • Manual inspection judged 69% of sampled ResNet-18 final-layer units meaningful, 31% polysemantic. Jesse Mu et al., 2020 ↗
  • Only a few hundred of 2048 ResNet-50 penultimate neurons look polysemantic by CLIP clustering. Maximilian Dreyer et al., 2024 ↗
  • Under 1% of TabPFN neurons are significantly salient and selective for any predefined ICD concept. Ricardo Knauer et al., 2026 ↗
Rule-like neurons in small game- and task-trained models 7 studies

In small game- or task-trained models such as OthelloGPT, a substantial share of neurons in some layers act as rule-like detectors. Their causal roles are pattern-specific but incomplete.

  • Depth-4 decision trees on board features predict 913 of 2,048 layer-5 OthelloGPT neurons well. Aditya Singh et al., 2025 ↗
  • 610 OthelloGPT neurons predict move-enabling board patterns with F1 above 0.99. jylin04, 2024 ↗
  • Keeping only OthelloGPT neurons for active tile-flipping rules mostly did no better than ablating all neurons. JMaar, 2025 ↗
No single scale or fine-tuning trend in monosemanticity 6 studies

Proxy measures of neuron monosemanticity show no single trend with scale or fine-tuning: some fall with scale, some show no ordering by model size, and neurons matched across models grow more selective. DPO moves the measures up in some layers and down in others, and fine-tuning repurposes many pretrained vision neurons.

Apparent polysemanticity often reflects one coherent feature 10 studies

Many neurons that look polysemantic respond to one coherent shared feature, such as word form, pose variants, shifted motifs, related concepts or slices of one manifold. Vision neurons that mix related concepts share more upstream circuitry than those mixing unrelated ones, so polysemanticity estimates can overstate how much unrelated concepts are mixed.

  • Across nine transformers, one word's different senses share more MLP neurons than synonyms do. Iyad Ait Hou et al., 2026 ↗
  • ResNet-50 channel 1721 fires on dog faces, with weakly distinct pose or viewpoint subclusters. Manan Gupta et al., 2025 ↗
  • Pythia expert neurons are polysemantic along interpretable lines: 'cat' overlaps 'dog', not 'car'. Masha Fedzechkina et al., 2025 ↗

Top-example explanations only partly predict full activation range 41 studies

Explanations built from top-activating examples, for SAE features, vision units and some neurons, only partly predict activation on held-out, lower-range, near-neighbour, out-of-domain or inhibitory inputs. Active hypothesis testing improves them.

  • Least-activating patches suppress most final-layer units in three vision models; top-patch explanations miss this. Robin Hesse et al., 2026 ↗
  • GPT-2 SAE explanations from top examples alone score worse than ones sampled across activations. kh4dien, 2024 ↗
  • Iterative hypothesis testing lifts SAE-feature simulation correlations from 0.52–0.70 to 0.65–0.83. Jiao-Jiao Han et al., 2025 ↗

Neurons versus dictionary features

These findings compare native neurons with SAEs and other learned dictionaries on interpretability, reproducibility and coverage, and show how dictionary features themselves vary. They help decide whether to analyse neurons or dictionary features.

Dictionary feature quality varies with design, layer and domain 81 studies

How interpretable, pure and stable SAE and other dictionary features (not neurons) are depends on domain, layer and design choices such as L0, width, architecture and supervision. Automated scores range from below permutation controls to high, and dictionaries recover features imperfectly and vary by seed.

  • In toy models, SAE latents mix correlated features unless L0 is set correctly. David Chanin et al., 2025 ↗
  • On matched GPT-2 layer 7, absorption varies about 12-fold across SAE architectures. Seyed Arshan Dalili et al., 2026 ↗
  • Supervised TaxoSAE puts each higher-level taxonomy concept on about one latent; unsupervised SAEs spread it widely. Xin-Yu Guo et al., 2026 ↗
Dictionary features and other directions often beat native neurons 51 studies

Rated by the same tools or raters, sparse dictionary features and other non-neuron directions are more often interpretable, concept-selective or steerable than native neurons. This holds in language, vision, ECG and other scientific models, though much of the language-model evidence comes from small transformers.

  • SAE features beat top-k neurons on interpretability scores (fuzzing, detection) in Gemma 2 and Llama 3.1. Gonçalo Paulo et al., 2024 ↗
  • Crowdworkers gave Pythia-70M SAE features mean ratings of 53–82, neurons 30–36, out of 100. Samuel Marks et al., 2024 ↗
  • In vision-language-action models, 70–90% of SAE features were labelled interpretable, versus 30% of FFN neurons. Aiden Swann et al., 2026 ↗
Few units recur across seeds, architectures or dictionary fits 16 studies

Few individual neurons recur across seeds or architectures, and those that recur tend to be more interpretable. SAE features align across architectures better than raw neurons, but individual dictionary features often fail to reproduce across fits, sizes or paraphrases, while organisation-level patterns recur.

  • Only about 1–5% of neurons are universal across seeds; these usually have clear interpretations. Wes Gurnee et al., 2024 ↗
  • Best-matched Pythia and Mamba SAE features correlate 0.74 on average; raw neuron activations only 0.30. Junxuan Wang et al., 2024 ↗
  • Across SAEs sharing one initialization, only 11.3–13.9% of latents keep the same top-activating token position. Valentin Noël, 2026 ↗
Native neurons sometimes match or beat dictionary features 11 studies

On some 1-D probing and causal-localization tests, native neurons match or beat SAE and transcoder features, and much dictionary activity, especially in late layers, duplicates single MLP neurons. Neurons still miss some rare fine-grained concepts such as bigrams, and SAE reconstruction error can cost model knowledge.

  • The best single MLP neuron beats single SAE features on 1-D probes of 113 concepts. Grace Luo et al., 2026 ↗
  • Most SAEs fall below the GPT-2 small neuron basis on country disentanglement. Maheep Chaudhary et al., 2024 ↗
  • Transcoder features aligned to single neurons supply 38.0–82.8% of all feature activations. Stepan Shabalin et al., 2026 ↗
Concepts spread over groups of SAE latents 3 studies

Part-of-speech categories, physical-event attributes and legal factors are carried by groups of SAE latents rather than one latent per concept, and some latents serve two related categories. These are SAE features, not neurons.

  • In a LLaMA-3-8B SAE, part-of-speech tags map to compact latent groups that partly overlap. Alessandro Bondielli et al., 2026 ↗
  • In a GraphCast SAE, position and pressure relations use modest feature sets; intensity uses many features. Jia-Pei Liu et al., 2026 ↗

Steering and locating behaviour

These findings cover using selected neurons or dictionary features to steer or localize behaviour, and when single units fail as causal handles.

Small selected neuron sets control specific behaviours 52 studies

Small, statistically selected neuron sets, often under 1% of neurons, causally control or predict behaviours such as arithmetic, programming language, refusal, bias and knowledge recall, well beyond random-neuron controls. The pattern holds in LLMs, vision-language, audio-language and vision models, and several studies read these sets as population-level readouts rather than one neuron per job.

  • Ablating 320 arithmetic neurons cuts Llama3-8B addition accuracy to 0.3%; random ablation does not. Wen-Yu Du et al., 2025 ↗
  • Ablating language-specific neurons, under 0.7% of all, sharply cuts pass@3 in that programming language. Zhe Yin et al., 2025 ↗
  • Clamping top 2,500 safety neurons (5,000 for Qwen2.5-32B) cuts average attack success to at most 2.0%. Wei Zhao et al., 2026 ↗
Function is distributed across populations, not single units 34 studies

Neurons or features picked by correlation, selectivity or a well-scoring explanation are often causally insufficient, and aligned subspaces or populations beat single neurons. Neuron selections are only partly specific to the concept and dataset.

  • Toxic-labelled neurons explain only 0.41%–23.9% of DPO's toxicity reduction. Yu-Shi Yang et al., 2024 ↗
  • The neuron most correlated with evaluation awareness fails an ablation-based causal check at 3B and 8B. Deepanshu Mody et al., 2026 ↗
  • Toy Transformer: neuron patching reaches 0.59–0.73 interchange-intervention accuracy; learned rotated bases (DAS) reach at least 0.95. Chang Lu et al., 2026 ↗
Dictionary features as causal control units 33 studies

Selected SAE features and other dictionary units (not neurons) can act as concept-selective causal controls in language, diffusion, vision, weather, ECG, protocol and music models. The effects depend on the baseline, model and judge, can be undone by fine-tuning, and can produce out-of-distribution artifacts.

  • Removing concept-aligned SAE features cuts Qwen3-0.6B accuracy to 4.2%, against 46.8% for random. Genze Jiang et al., 2026 ↗
  • SAE ablation beats all-layer dense steering in Gemma-2-9B but loses to same-layer baselines. Da-Ming Luo, 2026 ↗
  • Fine-tuning on deceptive data undoes DistilGPT-2 deception-feature ablation within a few epochs. Eduard Kapelko, 2025 ↗

Other units, bases and layers

These findings cover attention heads, MoE experts, the neuron basis itself and layer depth, for choosing what to inspect and where.

Unit concepts and interpretability vary with layer depth 12 studies

The kind of concept a unit matches, and how well its explanation holds, change systematically with layer depth. No single direction holds across models and methods.

  • GAN generator: object and part units concentrate in layers 4–7, materials and shapes later. David Bau et al., 2019 ↗
  • In VGG-19, mammal concept-classifier F1 rises from 0.04 to about 0.8 with depth. Andong Wang et al., 2022 ↗
  • In robot policy π0.5, the share of motion-verb neurons rises from 25.8% to 40.3% with depth. Bryce Grant et al., 2026 ↗
Whole MoE experts as interpretable units 8 studies

Whole MoE experts can get short labels that predict held-out routing or activation moderately to well, more so under sparser routing. But the features routing to one expert span many semantic clusters, and expert labels are broader than those of their neurons.

  • In Qwen3-30B-A3B, routing each token to more experts makes expert-level explanations predict less accurately. Ming-Xue Tian et al., 2026 ↗
  • LLM labels for whole MoE experts mostly score F1 above 0.8 on held-out detection. Jeremy Herbst et al., 2026 ↗
  • Each MoE expert's best-predicting SAE latents span many semantic clusters: 10.8–11.3 of 20 on average. I. Lasy et al., 2026 ↗
Is the neuron basis privileged? It depends 8 studies

CNN unit interpretability drops under random rotation, and activation functions privilege their own basis. But BERT [CLS] and residual-stream bases are no more interpretable than random directions, and unit-level decompositions miss task or content/style separations that linear subspaces recover.

  • Randomly rotating AlexNet conv5's basis cuts unique concept detectors by 80%, with unchanged discriminative power. David Bau et al., 2017 ↗
  • Small autoencoders with tanh-like activations align latents with axes the activation favors, not random ones. George Bird, 2025 ↗
  • In tanh RNNs, linear decompositions find task-specific components that unit-level decompositions miss. Shreya Saha et al., 2026 ↗
Most attention heads do several jobs 7 studies

Most attention heads appear to do several jobs, some of them related, while a minority are monosemantic. Some head roles concentrate in a single direction.

  • Only 14 of 144 GPT-2 Small attention heads look plausibly monosemantic. Connor Kissane et al., 2024 ↗
  • Pythia-160M Lorsa heads (sparse attention decompositions) get LLM-simulation interpretability scores roughly matching attention-output SAE features. Zheng-Fu He et al., 2025 ↗
  • In late-layer OpenCLIP-L heads, the first principal component alone sometimes matches the whole head's text description. Lorenzo Basile et al., 2024 ↗

All 7 gaps
  • The essay's headline share, roughly 90% of Qwen3-32B neuron sides (positive or negative) with one main job, rests only on its own preliminary audit, which gives no sample size, judge or threshold. No outside audit of neuron sides in Qwen3-32B or a similar model has been found so far, and some studies that may report such shares have not yet been checked.
  • No neuron-level study above 100B parameters tests any of the claims; the only result at that scale, shown only in figures, is how well explanations of whole experts predict activity in the mixture-of-experts Qwen3-235B-A22B, and the essay's claim that its picture holds up to trillion-parameter models rests on one Kimi K3 graph with no ablation. Outside the essay, circuit evidence stops at about 9B, and no test of how well neuron explanations predict activations covers 10–100B.
  • No study compares neuron job shares between base and post-trained versions of a model, or across sizes within one family. Proxy measures disagree: one study of Pythia 70M–6.9B finds a proxy for monosemanticity (neurons with a single meaning) falling with scale, while another finds no ordering by size and a shift after preference tuning (DPO) that goes opposite ways in early and late layers.
  • Most outside evidence concerns GPT-2 XL, small language models, SAE features, vision units or narrow circuit tasks, not neurons of a modern large model. Architecture evidence comes mostly from vision CNNs, GANs, toy and purpose-built models, and how GLU gating affects neurons, compared with a plain feed-forward layer, has been tested only in a toy model and in small chess-playing transformers.
  • Some studies find dictionary features more interpretable than neurons, while others find native neurons match or beat them. Likewise, small selected neuron sets control behaviours in some studies while others find function spread across populations, and neither tension is resolved.
  • The claim that apparently polysemantic neurons hide one coherent feature rests on the essay alone. The only outside test looks at a different InceptionV1 neuron and points to superposition (features sharing neurons), and work on language-model neurons that detect word forms and on ResNet-50 channels has not yet been compared with it.
