Polysemantic neurons

Individual neurons respond strongly to apparently unrelated inputs. Olah et al., 2020 ↗

In brief

Independent studies find neurons that respond to unrelated inputs in image classifiers and language models. How common they are remains unsettled: judgments depend on activation range and scoring, and studies disagree about which language-model layers contain most. Some apparent mixtures resolve into shared or related features, but whether truly unrelated mixtures are rare is contested. Packing more sparse features into fewer neurons explains mixing in toy models, but is not its only cause. Causal explanations and architectural fixes remain tested mostly in toy or small networks, with mixed evidence that mixing can be reduced without substantial performance costs.

Papers to start with: Olah et al., 2020; Elhage et al., 2022; Mu et al., 2020

Results that bear on this claim without testing it as worded.

Units that recur across seeds or models are more interpretable 9 studies

Few neurons recur across seeds or models, and those that do tend to be more interpretable. Superposed features are arranged differently across seeds, and polysemanticity concentrates in units that do not recur.

  • 1.23-4.16% of GPT-2 and Pythia neurons recur across seeds; selected recurring ones usually have clear interpretations. Wes Gurnee et al., 2024 ↗
  • Toy models from different seeds arrange the same features in different superposition patterns. André Longon et al., 2025 ↗
  • Neuron interpretability scores predict recurrence across models in all 12 vision-model pairs, more weakly in LLMs. Junze Zhou et al., 2026 ↗
Units mix features even with spare capacity 7 studies

Units can mix several features even when capacity is ample. The reported drivers are the data distribution (for example, whether the latent variables behind the data can be negative and which values they can take), noise statistics, regularization and SGD itself.

  • In overcomplete toy MLPs, many specialized neurons still spread activation across clusters. Amirhesam Abedsoltan et al., 2026 ↗
  • With weight decay and one neuron per latent, signed or log-normal latents lose one-to-one neuron alignment. Junze Zhou et al., 2026 ↗
  • In biologically constrained networks, latent variables' ranges and support, not their dependence, govern neuron mixing. Will Dorrell et al., 2024 ↗

Decomposing beyond single neurons

For practitioners who swap neurons for learned directions such as sparse autoencoder latents, or split neurons into sub-units, these findings show how much cleaner those units get and where they still mix concepts.

Learned directions are more monosemantic than single neurons 39 studies

Across language, vision, audio, protein and other models, directions found by SAEs, sparse coding, PCA, k-means or NMF score as more monosemantic or interpretable than single neurons or basis dimensions. In at least one model, VGG16, they score about the same.

  • Llama 3.1 8b neurons score near random on explanation quality, well below trained SAEs. Gonçalo Paulo et al., 2024 ↗
  • In four of five CNNs, learned directions match their concept labels more precisely than single neurons. Alexandros Doumanoglou et al., 2025 ↗
  • In Qwen2-Audio, top SAE features score far more monosemantic than top residual dimensions. T. F. Chowdhury et al., 2026 ↗
SAE design choices change how monosemantic latents are 33 studies

Dictionary width and sparsity, encoder architecture, regularizers and concept supervision all shift how monosemantic sparse-dictionary latents are. The gains often come at some cost in reconstruction.

  • Off-support penalties raise Top-k vision SAE monosemanticity at matched reconstruction; one SigLIP2 setting barely moves. Nathanaël M. A. Jacquier et al., 2026 ↗
  • Matryoshka SAEs show far less first-letter absorption than larger BatchTopK SAEs. Bart Bussmann, 2024 ↗
  • A Parity Bottleneck shows far less absorption than TopK or Matryoshka SAEs, but lower automated-interpretability scores. Andrew Mack et al., 2026 ↗
Applied SAE latents are mostly, not fully, monosemantic 26 studies

SAE latents trained on vision, pathology, recommender, EEG and language models are largely but not fully monosemantic. Labels drawn from top activations can misstate what a latent does causally.

  • A pathologist judged most pathology-SAE concepts monosemantic: 75% on Camelyon16, 86% on PANDA. Su-Su Sun et al., 2025 ↗
  • Most vision SAE configurations score near-maximal Connectivity (feature overlap), which the authors read as polysemanticity. Teodor Chiaburu et al., 2026 ↗
  • Deactivating CLIP SAE gender latents barely moves gender skew, while age latents lower it. Na-Min An et al., 2026 ↗
SAE latent scores depend on activation range and evaluation setup 24 studies

Whether a sparse-dictionary latent looks monosemantic depends on which activations are inspected, since many look coherent only at the top, and on the scoring metric, embedding space and evaluation harness.

  • In gelu-2l, attention SAE features are specific at high activations but polysemantic lower down. Connor Kissane, 2024 ↗
  • Embedding-based monosemanticity scores rise the more alike the scoring encoder's embeddings are: CLIP highest, DINOv3 lowest. Katarzyna Filus et al., 2026 ↗
  • Tightly capping the judge model's replies can collapse protein SAE odd-one-out scores; prompt wording shifts them. Piotr Jedryszek et al., 2026 ↗
Absorption, hedging and co-occurrence make SAE latents polysemantic 20 studies

Sparse-dictionary latents become polysemantic through feature absorption, hedging and concept co-occurrence. This happens both when the dictionary is too narrow or too sparse and when width suffices but the sparsity objective favours merging.

  • A Gemma Scope 'starts with S' latent doesn't fire on 'short'; a token-specific latent absorbs it. David Chanin et al., 2024 ↗
  • Feature hedging falls steeply with SAE width but stays nonzero at width 65536. David Chanin et al., 2025 ↗
  • In GPT-2 and Pythia-410M SAEs, 18-36% of active features don't distinguish the senses of polysemous words. Iyad Ait Hou et al., 2026 ↗
Polysemantic neurons can be split into cleaner sub-units 9 studies

A polysemantic neuron's distinct responses can run through separable upstream inputs, input weights or downstream heads. Splitting along these paths yields more monosemantic sub-units without changing model outputs.

  • Splitting selected CNN channels by previous-layer inputs yields two class-specific channels. Robin Hesse et al., 2025 ↗
  • Rewiring an already trained ViT splits units into more semantically coherent subunits, leaving accuracy unchanged. Dogukan Bagci et al., 2026 ↗
  • CLIP-ResNet neuron contributions span more than one direction; each neuron-attention-head pair's contribution spans one. Edmund Bu et al., 2025 ↗

Editing, ablation and robustness

What superposition and shared units mean when you ablate, edit or protect neurons, or try to harden a model against attacks and noise.

Interpretable features spread across many neurons 21 studies

In trained networks, interpretable features mostly do not line up with single neurons. Each spreads over many neurons, so ablating or protecting neurons does not single out one feature.

  • In gelu-1l, about 92% of SAE feature decoder vectors are dense in the neuron basis. Neel Nanda, 2023 ↗
  • In Gemma-2 9B-it, a median MLP neuron's output draws on 500-1350 SAE features. Evan Ning et al., 2026 ↗
  • In AlexNet fc1, 80% of activation variance needs 42 PCs but 2782 neurons. Nolan Dey et al., 2020 ↗
Units chosen for one task also serve others 12 studies

Neurons or parameters selected for one task, concept or behaviour also contribute to others, so ablating or editing them has off-target effects. One study finds the shared neurons peripheral.

  • Unrelated knowledge statements in one LLM share up to 18% of activated neurons. Shiqi Wang et al., 2025 ↗
  • In a multitask model, conditioning on one unit's ablation-mask marginal cuts task entropy by about 4.21%; conditioning on the full mask cuts it by 82.6% on average. Andrew Nam et al., 2025 ↗
  • Counterpoint: ablating all 26 neurons shared between SmolLM3-3B behaviour circuits had no effect. Hong-Liang Liu et al., 2026 ↗
Superposition linked to adversarial and noise vulnerability 12 studies

Superposition raises vulnerability to adversarial attacks and noise and can cause behavioural errors. Adversarial training can reduce it by dropping low-amplitude features, but whether it rises or falls under adversarial training depends on task demand relative to capacity.

  • Under label noise, SAE and non-negative contrastive features beat contrastive ones by up to 13.7 points. Qi Zhang et al., 2024 ↗
  • Adversarial (PGD) perturbations align closely with feature-interference directions; correlated features make attacks transfer across seeds. Edward Stevinson et al., 2025 ↗
  • An adversarially trained toy model drops exactly the 70 low-amplitude non-robust features. Adam Elimadi, 2026 ↗

Known gaps in this draft

How common polysemantic neurons are and whether they mix unrelated concepts remain unsettled, the canonical InceptionV1 neuron has not been independently re-examined, causal accounts and architectural fixes rest mostly on toy and small models, and several central works are not yet covered.

All 7 gaps
  • How common polysemantic neurons are in language models is unsettled: the two counts use different definitions and disagree, with under 5% of neurons per layer in Pythia-70M and GPT-2 Small (by a sparse-autoencoder criterion) against 46.9% in Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct (by one LLM judge). Studies also disagree on which layers are most polysemantic, and no model above 100B parameters has a count.
  • Whether polysemantic neurons mostly mix related or unrelated concepts is open. The main random-sample estimate covers one ResNet-18 layer under a lenient criterion, and the language-model evidence is mostly qualitative, some of it from the original authors' group.
  • The canonical InceptionV1 neuron 4e:55 has not been independently re-examined. Studies of Inception models each use a different criterion for 'unrelated', so no share of polysemantic units in InceptionV1 can be stated.
  • Evidence that superposition causes polysemanticity comes mainly from toy models. In real networks it rests on few studies, and comparisons of sparse-autoencoder features with neurons are correlational.
  • Polysemanticity with at least as many neurons as features has been shown only in toy models, mainly with an L1 activation penalty that is uncommon in practice. Other toy studies find that when neurons exactly match features in number and weight decay is used, mixing depends on the data distribution, so the role of regularization is unresolved.
  • Evidence that architecture or training changes cut polysemanticity without a large loss comes from toy models and from small or unstated-size networks, and one CNN study reports no accuracy for its sparsity-penalty runs. A language model designed to have interpretable neurons has worse loss than GPT-2, no other group has re-measured SoLU's interpretable-neuron rate, and a chess-model study and toy-model studies give mixed results on SoLU.
  • Several central works are not yet among the studies drawn on here: Feature Visualization (2017), Multimodal Neurons (2021), Polysemanticity and Capacity in Neural Networks (2022), Towards Monosemanticity (2023) and Bills et al. (2023) on LLM-written neuron explanations. Any of them could change the picture on prevalence and on superposition as a cause.
